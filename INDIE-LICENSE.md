# GraphSCII Indie Commercial Permission

**Indies: just ask.**

GraphSCII is **Graphical Standard for Computer Information Interchange**, created and designed by **Ariel Williams**.

The normal public license is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 for noncommercial use. If your project is commercial but genuinely small and indie, the intended policy is simple:

> Email me and ask. It will probably be free, or very inexpensive.

## Who this is for

This path is intended for people such as:

- solo developers;
- tiny game or software teams;
- hobbyists selling a small project;
- game-jam projects that later become commercial;
- independent artists and writers;
- small open-source projects with a modest paid component;
- student or educational projects that later earn money; and
- early-stage projects with little or no meaningful revenue yet.

There is deliberately no rigid revenue cutoff in this document. Context matters more than forcing every creator into an arbitrary tier.

## What to send

Contact Ariel Williams using the public contact method listed with the GraphSCII project or Ariel Williams’s public GitHub profile.

A request can be as short as:

```text
Subject: GraphSCII indie permission — <project name>

Hi Ariel,

I would like to use GraphSCII in <project>.

What it is: <one or two sentences>
Team size: <number>
Commercial model: <paid / donations / ads / Patreon / etc.>
Expected scale: <roughly>
How GraphSCII is used: <brief description>

May I have indie commercial permission?
```

That is enough to start.

## What happens if Ariel says yes

This file is **not an automatic commercial license**. You still need a written yes.

If Ariel Williams replies in writing that you may use GraphSCII commercially for the project you described, that written permission may serve as your indie commercial license for that project, subject to any conditions stated in the reply.

The permission may be:

- free;
- a small one-time amount;
- attribution-only;
- conditioned on keeping GraphSCII modifications open; or
- another simple arrangement that fits the project.

If the project later becomes substantially larger, is acquired, is sublicensed, becomes a major commercial product, or changes materially from what you described, ask again so the commercial permission can be updated fairly.

## Spirit of the indie policy

GraphSCII is meant to be used.

Study it. Draw with it. Modify it. Share it. Keep forks open.

The commercial restriction is not meant to make life difficult for tiny creators. It is meant to prevent companies from commercially exploiting the work without permission while leaving a very easy door open for small creators who simply ask.
